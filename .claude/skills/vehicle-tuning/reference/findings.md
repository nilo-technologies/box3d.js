# Findings

What tuning the 45 car matrix taught, with the measurement that showed it. Newest at the bottom. Add to this file when you learn something new.

## Testing

1. **The lateral grip test must turn off the speed sensitive steering.** With `speedReference` 11 m/s the steering at 70 km/h is capped at about 8°, and the front tires need about 7° of slip at the peak, so lateral g measured the steering, not the tires: the arcade sports car read 1.17 g, less than the 50% car (1.32 g). With `speedReference` 0 during the test it reads 1.61 g and the realism ordering holds.
2. **The chassis is one box with no cabin.** A car dropped on its side tips back onto its wheels without any self righting, because the box is only 0.5-0.7 m tall. Test self righting from the roof instead.
3. **A dead level roof leaves the righting direction undecided.** At exactly 180° the roll angle flips between +π and -π every step and the torques cancel, so even `selfRighting` 10 stayed on its roof. Tilt the roof test by about 6°, as a real landing would be.
4. **Handbrake tests need full steer.** At 0.4 steer and 60 km/h every level rotated at about 33°/s, which is just cornering. At full steer the realistic sports car peaks at 78°/s and the arcade one at 170°/s.
5. **Lift before braking.** Lifting the throttle at 6000 rpm sends a -1000 N·m engine braking pulse into the driven wheels. Landing it in the same frames as full brake pushed the front wheels of a FWD supercar past their peak slip, and ABS never recovered them (37.4 m instead of 34.3 m). The metrics lift for 0.25 s first, like a driver.

## Engine behavior to design around

6. **The automatic gearbox shifts on road rpm, which trails the engine.** The shift uses the lower of engine rpm and wheel speed through the gearing. At full load the clutch slips about 300 rpm, so with `shiftUpRpm` 8200 and `maxRpm` 8500 the supercar sat on the limiter in 5th at 277 km/h. At 7900 it shifts and reaches 338 km/h. Keep `shiftUpRpm` at least 5% under `maxRpm` (and see #24: the shift points are now at 90%).
7. **ABS cannot release a locked wheel.** ABS caps the brake at the tire's peak torque, but a locked wheel needs only the lower sliding torque to stay locked, so a wheel that overshoots its peak slip (a full brake step before the weight has moved forward, or an engine braking pulse) stays locked. A front brake far above the front tires' peak makes this likely: the arcade AWD supercar braked in 24.3 m with 3200 N·m fronts and 22.2 m with 2400 N·m. Size front brakes near 1.15x the front peak torque on cars with ABS. (This is an engine limitation worth fixing in box3d: ABS should release when the slip passes the peak.)
8. **Traction control is conservative on a launch.** It caps the engine at the driven tires' peak torque, but part of that torque spins up the engine's own inertia and some goes to engine friction, so in first gear the tire ran at about 60% of its peak with no slip at all. The 50% rally car with TC took 6.47 s to 100 km/h while the 75% one without it (and with less grip) took 5.27 s. Lower engine inertia helps a little (0.15 to 0.1: 6.50 to 6.25 s). Expect two wheel drive cars with TC to launch slower than the tires allow.
9. **Full throttle without TC spins the driven tires to about 50 km/h** on a 420 N·m RWD car (slip ratio 7 at launch), so the tire works at its sliding friction. That is the realistic result for a flat out launch without launch control.
10. **The yaw inertia comes from the collision box.** `mass` rescales the shapes' inertia, so a longer or wider `halfExtents` makes the car rotate more lazily. Moving the center of mass does not change the inertia.
11. **Downforce acts at the center of mass** with forward speed squared: it adds grip at both ends equally and cannot balance the car. It also adds rolling resistance (the top speed fell 2-3% at the arcade levels).

## Tuning

12. **Body roll**: 22000/18000 N/m bars on a 1400 kg car with 2.0/2.2 Hz springs gave 1.3° in a 0.9 g step steer (too stiff for a road car); 16000/13000 gave 1.7°.
13. **FWD needs weight on the front.** Moving the center of mass 0.15 m forward took the FWD sports car from 7.05 s to 6.32 s and the realistic FWD roll into range.
14. **counterSteer fights the handbrake.** Above about 0.6 it catches the slide faster than `driftGripScale` lets the rear go: arcade rotated less than 25% until counterSteer went 0.7 to 0.6 and driftGripScale 0.45 to 0.4.
15. **A taller body needs more self righting.** `selfRighting` 3 rights the sports car (box half height 0.28 m) from its roof but not the rally car (0.34 m); 4 does both.
16. **Robustness check**: after tuning, ±3% mass and ±2% grip on all 45 presets flipped no checks. Run a similar check after big changes, because ABS lockups and spins are threshold effects and a preset that only just passes can flip.
17. **Only about 58% of the engine power reaches the drag at top speed.** Sports car: 236 kW at 6301 rpm in 6th against 139 kW of drag at 253 km/h. Supercar: 476 kW against 274 kW at 323 km/h. Rolling resistance (raised by downforce), wheel and engine damping and clutch slip take the rest. The plain (P ÷ drag)^(1/3) estimate overshot by 15-20% in the blind tests.
18. **Blind test, first round** (three agents with only this skill): a 50% AWD rally car and an arcade RWD supercar passed every check on the first run. A 75% FWD hot hatch failed braking on the first run (36.5 m against 36.4 m, with 2400 N·m front brakes, about 1.55x the front peak) and then sat on the top speed floor; it took six runs. That led to the brake sizing rule against the level's grip, the 0.58 power factor, the gear and rpm in the top speed output, `--perturb`, and gear ratios for every archetype.
19. **Blind test, second round** (fresh agents, same briefs, the skill after round one): all three cars passed every check on the first measurement and every perturbed variant (95/95, 95/95, 75/75). The top speed estimate landed within 1 km/h for the supercar and the hatch. Round two found that the brake rule was too cautious as a cap (a rally car stopped 2.1 m shorter with the archetype's 2300 N·m than with 1550), so it is now a starting point that the measurement adjusts.
20. **After merging the box3d update** (hit material friction, touchdown load, reverse traction control, brake to reverse): all 45 presets still pass, but one perturbed variant does not. The 100% FWD supercar with 2% less grip brakes in 35.1 m against 35.0. Its driven front wheels lock at the brake step (findings #7), and no preset knob changed that: front brake 2100-2400 N·m, tire peak slip 0.09-0.11, engine inertia and damping, clutch strength, shift points and wheel inertia all left it within 0.3 m. It needs the ABS fix in box3d, not tuning. New `assists.brakeReverse` (default on) makes the brake select reverse once stopped with the automatic gearbox; the metrics stop measuring before that.
21. **Sharp bumps are an engine limit, not a tuning one.** The track's old speed bumps were 14 cm capsule ridges. Every car took 10-18 g spikes at 30 km/h, bottoming its suspension, and at 45-60 km/h most were launched or flipped, rally and off-roader included. The suspension pushes along the contact normal, which on the face of a sharp ridge tilts about 45°, so the hit kicks the car up and back at once. No setting fixed 45 km/h and up. A real speed hump (8 cm high, 3.7 m long, rounded) gives 0.5-1.3 g at up to 60 km/h on every preset, so the track now has those. Test bump comfort on hump shaped obstacles; a sharp ridge measures the engine's contact model.
22. **Lower compression damping than rebound** (sports 0.3/0.75, super 0.35/0.8, rally 0.3/0.7) took the stiff cars' hump peaks at 60 km/h from 0.83 to 0.65 g (sports) and 1.27 to 1.01 g (super) with every metric still passing.
23. **Keep `minLength` well above `radius - (box half height - wheel y)`.** Shortening the sports car's `minLength` from 0.17 to 0.07-0.12 m to make room for bumps made the 100% car roll off its roof: upside down, the wheel's ground probe starts close to the roof line, overlaps the ground under it, and the suspension pushes the car over. Raising `maxLength` for room also lowers the automatic center of mass relative to the body (it follows the axle), which can put it below the box.
24. **Shift at 90% of the limiter, measured on the engine.** Racing games upshift at about 90% of the rev limit. With `shiftUpRpm` set to 0.9 × `maxRpm`, the engine still reached 94-96% before each shift, because the gearbox reads road rpm and the clutch slips 270 rpm (sports, rally) to 490 rpm (supercar) at full load; the old 6900 rpm setting on the sports car shifted at 99%. Settings of 6200, 6500 and 7150 rpm put every gear's shift at 90.1-90.4% of 7200, 7500 and 8500. The metrics `shiftShare` check now measures it. A car without TC spinning its tires in first gear still hits the limiter first: the gearbox holds the upshift until the driven wheels grip.
25. **A limited slip front axle pulls under wheelspin.** Shifting earlier left the 100% FWD sports car (no TC) spinning its fronts into second gear, and with a 1.15 front LSD it veered 5.8 m over a 200 m flat out run (the smoke test allows 5). Open (1.0) it runs within 0.2 m; 1.3 gives 3.9 m. The rally FWD cars (1.3) drift 3.5 m at 100% and 1.4 m at 75%, inside the limit. The pull is always to the same side, so part of it may be solver order rather than physics.
26. **Race tracks: what made cars crash that was not their tuning** (`examples/src/vehicle-tracks.js`, checked by `examples/scripts/vehicle-laps.mjs`). A landscape averaged over 30 m around a steep, twisting road left a 45 degree bank 6 m beside a hairpin; averaging over 12 m near the road (40 m further out) keeps the ground within 15 m of the road under 35%. A crest 30 m up passing within 50 m of the start straight made a 22 m cliff between them, so keep high sections away from low ones. Bumps that stop at the road edge are walls to a car rejoining from the verge: ramp them down beyond it. A short, steep ledge (25 cm over 1.5 m) launches cars into whatever follows: give ramps 2 m or more and leave landing room. Off the end of the terrain cars fall forever: bank the edge and wall it. With those fixed, all 45 presets lap both tracks without flipping, at the autopilot's pace and 6% either side of it (`--pace 0.8` and `--pace 0.9`; the default is 0.85), taking the stage's bump section at 60 km/h.
27. **Slip ratio near rest needs relaxation.** The slip ratio divides the wheel and ground speed difference by the ground speed, so 0.05 s after full throttle the 100% RWD sports car's rears read 33x their peak slip at 0.6 km/h. `tire.longitudinalRelaxationLength` 0.3 m makes the same launch read 0.3x, 1.5x, 3.2x over the first 0.2 s. It does not stop wheelspin on a car with twice its rear grip in first gear (still spins to 78 km/h without TC), and allows 5-10% more throttle before the tires break loose. The relaxation must settle at a minimum speed (1 m/s): with the driven floor of 0.1 m/s a slip left from a spike at 1 km/h took seconds to decay, and launch control read it as wheelspin and cut the drive for 0.4 s.
28. **Launch control** (on by default). Full throttle from rest, before and after: sports-100-rwd spun to 79 km/h, 5.65 s to 100 km/h, now no wheelspin and 5.00 s; sports-100-fwd 92 km/h, 6.60 s, now 5.85 s; rally-100-rwd 5.65 s, now 5.15 s; super-100-rwd (TC on, no spin) 3.62 s, now 3.17 s; the FWD example (TC on) 9.68 s, now 7.98 s. Worst slip 1.0-1.5x the peak. Three designs failed first: an integrating cap (overshot to 7-10x the peak slip and launched slower than TC), a cap up to 2x the peak torque at the wheel (a light wheel spins up within one step on torque past the tire's peak), and ending at the first upshift (the gearbox cuts the throttle during a shift). After it, `--all --perturb` fails 7 of 4329 checks: super-0-fwd is now quicker than its target (0-100 3.82-3.93 s against a 4.00 s floor, in the preset and four perturbed variants), super-50-fwd rolled once on the rough ground test (its perturbed variants pass, and it rolls at 12.6 s, long after launch control ended at 4.4 s: the quicker start puts it on a different line through the bumps), and the known super-100-fwd grip-2% braking (#20).
29. **The 0-100 targets after launch control.** The 0.6 s two wheel drive slack is gone: it stood for the time lost without launch control, and with it the slowest two wheel drive car (sports-50-rwd grip-2%) runs 5.32 s against a 5.40 s ceiling. The FWD supercar floor at 100% is 4.7 s instead of 5.0: with the tires at their peak the 100% preset runs 6.2 s and the arcade one 3.8-3.9 s, under the old arcade floor of 4.0 s (5.0 × 0.8).
30. **Braking in a turn spun the 50% and arcade cars.** A keyboard player brakes and steers at once, so the metrics now run full brake and full lock from 110 km/h (`brakeTurn`). Before this, sports-50 reached 110-116° of sideslip, sports-25 and arcade 155-162°, even with ABS on and the steering limiter of the examples. The 100% car stayed at 9°. Two causes, both measured per wheel. ABS caps the brake at the tire's straight line peak and ignores the cornering force, so in a turn the front wheels lock within 0.3 s at every level (slip ratio -1), and the inside rear locks next. Then the outside rear is all that holds the car, and `rollInfluence` takes load off it: on sports-50 at 0, 0.1, 0.2 and 0.35 the sideslip was 7°, 11°, 47° and 116°. Moving the rear brake toward the ideal split (1500 to 900 N·m on the sports car, about 26% of the braking) and scaling it with the level's grip, with `rollInfluence` at 0.1-0.25 instead of 0.15-0.75, holds every sports preset under 20°. Braking distances moved by under 0.5 m. Lowering the rear brake alone was not enough at 25% (132° at 900 N·m with 0.55 `rollInfluence`), and 550 N·m cost 5 m of braking on the FWD and AWD cars. The real fix is ABS that leaves room for the cornering force, as traction control already does (`lateralRoom` in `vehicle.c`).
31. **counterSteer works in every corner, so it costs turning.** It adds the rear axle's slide angle to the steer target, and in a normal corner the rears run at 3-5° of slip angle, so 0.6 took about 2.5° off the 7° the fronts needed: the arcade car turned at 0.8 g at full lock and 120 km/h with 1.6 g of grip. Without it (0) the RWD 50% car spun in the step steer at 80 km/h. Stability from the tires works better than counter steer (see #33 for which kind). `counterSteer` was also what straightened a car after a handbrake slide, so with less of it the handbrake recovery test (not a metric) peaks at 25-45° instead of 12-20°, and still recovers on its own.
32. **75% without traction control is on a knife edge.** sports-75-rwd spun in the slalom (180°) with a rear grip bias of 1.05 and held 13° with 1.1; with no bias and grip 1.15 it spins in the flick (180°). Its full throttle step steer at 80 km/h spins in some settings and not others; the level may spin, so the metrics only check it at 50% and below. Check 75% RWD cars by hand with the keyboard moves.
33. **A rear only grip bias stabilises the car but kills drifts.** Rear lateral friction ×1.15-1.2 and a rear peak angle ×0.75-0.8 kept every keyboard move under 6° on the 50% sports car with 30-40% more steering at speed, but cut the handbrake rotation by 20% at 50% (148° to 117°, 134 to 108°/s) and 15% at 25%. Dividing `driftGripScale` by the bias won back only half, because the grippier rear straightens the car as soon as the handbrake is released. More grip on all four tires keeps the balance and the drifts (sports-50-rwd 147°, 133°/s; arcade 170°, 209°/s against 170°/s before). With no bias, more steering at speed is only safe from 25% down: at 50% ×1.15 the RWD car spun lock to lock (49-52°), and at arcade ×1.2 with grip 1.55 it spun in the flick (51°). Arcade grip 1.6 with ×1.15 holds every perturbed variant under 12°.
34. **Braking and steering together was set by ABS, not the presets.** With full brake and full lock the front wheels of every sports preset stayed locked for 93-96% of the stop, ABS on, so the car ran wide (1.0-1.7°/m of turn from 110 km/h). Front brakes ×0.8 changed nothing; ×0.65 and ×0.5 unlocked them and doubled the turn, but the car then spun (60-75° of sideslip), because the braked rears had no grip left for the cornering force, and weaker rear brakes as well (×0.5, ×0.35) still spun from 110 km/h. One brake torque cannot be right for a straight stop and a braking turn, so ABS in `vehicle.c` changed (#35).
35. **ABS with cornering room, release and engine braking.** Three parts, each found by measuring. (a) The ABS cap is the peak torque times the room on the friction ellipse, sqrt(1 - use²) with a 30% floor. (b) A wheel past its peak slip under braking is released to half its sliding torque, as traction control already does for wheelspin; this ends the lockups of #7 and #20. (c) Engine braking on a driven wheel shares the cap: without it the 50% RWD sports car braking mid corner spun at 124°, because the service brake was capped but the rears still carried 2300 N of engine braking. The cornering use must come from the slip angle (against `lateralPeakAngle`), not from last step's lateral force: a wheel braking hard carries little lateral force, which read as room to brake harder, and the arcade RWD supercar's rear braking force swung between 3000 and 10700 N and spun it at 168°. With all three, the fronts of every sports preset never lock in a full brake at full lock (0% of the stop), the car turns 60-80% more per metre (sports-50: 1.46 to 2.32°/m from 110 km/h) at 12-15° of sideslip, and straight line ABS braking is unchanged (the engine test: 32.2 to 32.1 m). Weaker front brakes on top of it spun the car again (×0.65: 50-70°), so the preset brakes stay. All 45 presets lap both tracks at paces 0.8 and 0.9; at 0.85 one 100% sports car rolls on the gravel stage, at that pace only (0.82-0.88 pass): it brakes for the bump section from about 98 km/h and lands on two wheels. Which one moves with every change (FWD after the ABS change, RWD after #37), so treat it as the autopilot meeting that section at a knife edge, not as a car that rolls.
36. **The steering limiter decides the balance, so test with it.** The examples cap the steer angle at the front tires' peak slip angle (now `createSteeringLimiter` in `box3d.js/wheeled-vehicles`). The WASD driver (`vehicle-wasd.mjs`) on the old presets with that cap: every level from 75% FWD down failed by ploughing wide at 5-10° of sideslip, and the arcade sports car was clean to 1.67 g against 2.0-2.2 g without the cap. Without it full lock at speed takes the fronts far past their peak (19° in a braking turn), and that understeer is what kept the cars from spinning. Holding the fronts at their best grip moved the balance to oversteer: the 25% RWD car braking at full lock went from 14° to 102° of sideslip through a cap at 1.5 times the peak. So the keyboard moves in the metrics now run through the limiter.
37. **WASD tuning of the sports car.** Measured as the sum of the clean pace (mean over three driver styles) over 10 presets, starting at 14.10 with the cap at 1.5. Rear tire side grip ×1.05 (`rear.tire`) made every keyboard move pass through the cap; ×1.1 made the 50% cars plough. `speedReference` 11 to 14 and lateral slide friction 0.94 to 1.02 (on both axles, the rear ×1.05 on top) raised it most (+1.3 together); `speedReference` 17 spun the cars braking into a turn. The cap at 1.0 passed every keyboard move but ploughed (13.2); at 1.25 and up braking at full lock spun the car (30-70°): a cap of 1.5, falling to 1.0 while braking (`brakingPeakScale`), did both (15.7). A realism row multiplier on the steering falloff (×1.15 at 25% and arcade) failed the slalom and flick and was dropped. Traction control on the car (it was off) took the 100% and 75% RWD cars from 1.07 and 1.27 g to 1.23 and 1.43 g, and their failure from a spin to a slide, with every check still passing. `rearGrip` 0.975 at arcade (0.985 at 25%) made the overspeed RWD car slide (16-29°) instead of plough; 0.97 failed one perturbed flick (26°), 0.98 ploughed again. Over the 15 sports presets the clean paces are now 1.10-1.23 g at 100%, 1.33-1.43 at 75%, 1.53-1.63 at 50%, 1.77-1.97 at 25% and 2.00-2.17 at arcade.
38. **What barely moves a keyboard car's cornering.** On top of #37, each within 0.1 g of the 15.93 sum (the noise): anti-roll bars 20000/13000 and 12000/10000 (from 16000/13000), springs 2.3/2.5 Hz (from 2.0/2.2), the center of mass 4 cm lower, steering lock 30° (from 34°), steering rates 5/7 rad/s (from 4/6), 470 N·m (from 420) and a 3.9 final drive (from 3.6). Tire grip is linear in load in this engine, so moving load between the axles or across them does not shift the grip balance the way it does on real tires; tune the balance with the tires.
39. **The rally car with the keyboard recipe.** WASD sum (15 presets) 18.80 before. Rear tire side grip ×1.05 fixed six of the seven lift-off spins through the limiter but turned less (18.37, two `turnsAtSpeed` failures); `speedReference` 12 to 15 and slide friction 0.90 to 0.96 gave the turning back (20.5) but left the RWD cars spinning braking mid corner and in the flick (32-47°). Neither the rear brake (1680 to 1150) nor the rear differential alone fixed that. A rear tire that peaks earlier (9° against 11°) and keeps its grip past the peak (slide friction = peak) did (367/369). It then read 1.21 g on the 100% RWD car in the lateral test, over the 1.13 ceiling, and 1.13 against 1.07 at 75% broke the realism ordering: the rear differential at 1.6 spun the inside rear on power, which yawed the car and inflated the reading. At 1.15 it read 1.02 g and at 1.1 every perturbed variant passed. Final: 1728/1728 with `--perturb`, WASD 20.94. A smaller rear bias (×1.02-1.03) made the overdone arcade car slide (15-25°) but failed the flick in 8-13 perturbed variants, so the arcade rally car runs wide at 10-14° of sideslip when overdone; its handbrake rotates it at about 200°/s.
40. **The supercar.** WASD sum 25.73 before. Its rear brake was 48% of the braking (2200/2400); 1300 N·m stopped the spins braking into a turn but braked 0.5 m long at arcade, 1500 does both. Rear tire side grip ×1.05 fixed the six lift-off spins; slide friction 1.0 to 1.15 and `speedReference` 13 to 15 took it to 28.03. Over the sweep's top (2.4 g) the arcade supercar never fails; taken far too fast (2.6-3.3 g) it ploughs at 6-10° of sideslip. A smaller rear bias brings the slides back (×1.0: 19-51°, ×1.02: 16-26°), but the 25% and arcade RWD cars then spin when lifting mid corner (34-57°), and neither less engine braking (0.08 to 0.025), a looser rear differential (1.1) nor less downforce at the arcade levels (×6 to ×2) changed that trade: overdone and lifted, a slide is a slide. The rear bias stays ×1.05.
41. **A braking move must end when the car stops.** With S held the automatic gearbox selects reverse once stopped, and a car reversing reads as 180° of sideslip. The arcade supercar stops from 110 km/h within the 2.5 s braking-turn move and read 164-168° ("spun") though its sideslip never passed 5°. The keyboard moves now end when the car drops below 1 m/s.
42. **The stage lap at pace 0.85.** Now two presets roll on the stage at that pace only (sports-100-rwd and super-50-fwd, both at 347-362 m, a fast crest before the bump section, which starts at 810 m), each passing from 0.82 to 0.88 and at 0.8 and 0.9; super-50-fwd lands back on its wheels. Which presets trip moves with every tuning change, so it is the autopilot reaching that section at one particular speed, not a property of the cars.
43. **The 0-100 floor follows the grip.** It was the archetype floor × (1 - 0.2 × arcade), set when the arcade grip was 1.45. With the arcade grip at 1.6 the FWD supercar, whose launch is traction limited, ran 3.60-3.68 s against 3.76. The floor is now the archetype floor × grip^-0.6: at the old grips (1.1, 1.2, 1.32, 1.45) that gives 0.94, 0.90, 0.85 and 0.80, the old floors within 0.01; at the new ones 0.92, 0.85, 0.80 and 0.75. All 45 presets then pass every check with `--perturb`.
44. **The merged ABS and the air damping.** nilo-technologies/box3d#10 merged with a second commit: rolling resistance comes out of the ABS cap, and engine braking is capped inside the clutch solve, so the engine speed follows the torque the wheels take instead of the wheels being trimmed after the solve. The engine test's braking turn went from 45° to 48°, the WASD sum from 73.20 to 73.47, and straight braking stayed at 32.2 m. It also moved two rough ground rolls onto the supercars (super-50-awd at 0.22 upright, super-25-awd grip-2% at 0.10): each was thrown off a bump at 85-98 km/h and rolled with all four wheels in the air. `airAngularDamping` is what acts there. At 50%, 2.0 fixed the supercar but rolled three sports-50-rwd variants instead (0.05-0.09); 2.5 kept every 50% car and grip variant upright, and 3 at 25% fixed super-25-awd. All 45 presets then pass with `--perturb`. The laps still trip at single paces (268/270 to 269/270 depending on the value): super-50-rwd ended on its roof at the stage's 347 m crest at 0.85, sports-50-fwd rolled over the circuit's big hump (taken flat out by design) at 0.9, super-75-rwd at the stage's 371 m at 0.9. The cars reach that crest at 110-120 km/h now that they corner faster, and the autopilot does not slow for crests.
45. **Understeer at full lock, and suspension you can see (sports car).** A probe held full lock (D) through the steering limiter at 50, 80 and 110 km/h and compared each axle's slip angle with its peak angle. With the rear tires 5% grippier (#37) the fronts ran at 1.27-1.33× their peak and the rears at 0.84-0.92×, at every level from 75% down: the front washed out with grip left at the rear, about 2.7° of understeer. Taking the rear bias away tipped the RWD car straight into a power slide (rears at 2.8-5×) and failed 8-17 keyboard moves (×1.025 and ×1.0). Front slide friction 1.02 to 1.1 barely moved the 50% car: at 1.3× the peak the front is only 20% into its falloff. A rear tire with the front's grip (1.10) but an earlier peak (5.5° against 7°) and no falloff (slide = peak) did it, with front slide friction 1.06: the 50% RWD rear now works at 1.05-1.13× its peak against the front's 1.27×, every check passes, and the WASD sum rose from 24.20 to 25.13; overdone, the RWD cars slide. The understeer angle (steer minus wheelbase ÷ radius) is no guide here: a rear that peaks sooner runs smaller slip angles and reads as more understeer. AWD kept pushing (rears at 0.92×): `drive.awdRearGrip` 0.985 balances it (0.97 to 0.95 already slides; 0.98 left one perturbed arcade lift-off at 25°). FWD did too, and a rear grip cut of 2.5-7% made the arcade and 25% FWD cars spin when lifting mid corner (25-39°), so FWD keeps its push. Suspension: 1.5/1.65 Hz springs (from 2.0/2.2) on 0.15-0.38 m travel (from 0.17-0.33; the softer spring sags 11 cm, so maxLength rose to keep the ride height) doubled the pitch (+1.2°, -2.15° at 100% and 50%) and used 12 cm of travel instead of 6.5. With the bars at 10000/8000 the 25% and arcade levels rolled past their caps (2.2° and 1.6°); the bars at 20000/16000 and the center of mass 2 cm lower (+0.10) keep every level in range, perturbed too. Stiffer bars (21000-24000) made the 100% car roll back off its roof, as did a shorter maxLength with `suspension.preload`, which also left the car 4 cm lower than its rest length predicts: the roof test is a threshold on where the center of mass sits against the box.

48. **The mountain pass: what made it hard to drive that was not the cars** (`examples/src/vehicle-tracks.js`, the point to point `downhill` and `uphill`, checked with `vehicle-laps.mjs --keys`). Four track problems, each found by a car crashing. (a) Catmull-Rom through unevenly spaced control points (a Douglas-Peucker pass left gaps from 0.5 to 12 m) overshoots into loops of 5 m radius; the 12 m wide road mesh folded over at them (460 downward triangles) and a 100% sports car rolled at 60 km/h on one. Evenly spaced points (5 m) fixed it. (b) Each road row is level across and turns with the road, so on a graded corner the quad between two rows twists and its two triangles meet at a crease a few millimetres high. With one quad across the road the wheels crossed creases 17 times a second at 60 km/h, and the dampers turned 2-3 mm of suspension change per step into ±1700 N of load chatter (p99 3000 N a step; a box slope or a planar mesh ramp gave under 60 N). Twelve strips across (`road.strips`) took it to p99 300 N. The circuit's hilly half shows the same chatter (median 318 N a step); see #49 for why it keeps one quad. (c) A hairpin tighter than the verge (6 m centreline radius against 6 m out to the ramp edge) folds the inside rows; they now stop at 85% of the tightest radius within 3 m. (d) Guardrails: a wheel sticks out about 0.2 m past the chassis box, so against a rail its cylinder cast landed on the rail's top (up to 24000 N) and the car climbed it and stayed at 30° of roll. The rails are in their own collision category that the wheel casts skip (`wheelsIgnoreRails`), and their friction is 0.1 so a glancing car slides along. `maxSlopeAngle` cannot help: the rail's top is flat.
49. **Road strips against touchdown snags (the race circuit).** Splitting the circuit's road into strips cuts its chatter as on the mountain pass (sports-50-rwd on the hilly half, per-step wheel load change p50/p99: 1 strip 318/3880 N, 3 strips 69/1096, 5 54/682, 7 47/441, 6 per side with a centre seam 39/316), but every split adds seams along the road, and a chassis touching down on them snags: after the big hump, taken flat out by design, the cars land about 15 degrees nose down (the same launch with any layout), and over 315 laps (45 presets, paces 0.8, 0.82, 0.84, 0.85, 0.86, 0.88, 0.9) the laps that lost over 20 km/h in a single step at touchdown were 8 with one quad, 12-17 with odd strip counts and about 20 with a seam down the middle; laps under 0.7 upright 7 against 8-19, failures 1 against 0-4. The likely cause is the chassis box catching on internal mesh edges as it lands; a bare box slid nose down onto a flat mesh did not isolate it (it dug in and stopped on either layout, with no wheels holding the nose up). Both effects look like engine work rather than track work: the damper reacting to millimetre creases between triangles, and a box catching on internal edges. The chatter fails no circuit lap, so the circuit keeps one quad; the mountain pass, with no jumps and chatter that did spin cars, keeps 12 strips.
50. **Tire load sensitivity (`tire.loadSensitivity`, nilo-technologies/box3d#16, off on every preset).** Real tires grip less per newton as their load rises; in this engine grip was proportional to the load (#38). The new coefficient scales every friction coefficient by 1 - k × (load ÷ static load - 1), linear as in the Magic Formula (no transcendental functions, for determinism). Engine unit test (default car, half lock at 15 m/s, all the roll stiffness at one end): the radius with the bars at the front against the rear differed by 2% without it and 11% at k 0.3. On the presets, holding 60 km/h at about 10° of front wheel angle with the roll stiffness split 80/20 one way or the other: sports-50-rwd 8.2% (k 0), 10.9% (0.1), 13.8% (0.2), 17.0% (0.3); sports-100-rwd 7.4, 10.7, 14.3, 18.3% (the stiff front always runs wider). It costs grip, as weight transfer does on real tires: at k 0.2 lateral g -5% (sports-50-rwd 1.43 to 1.36 g), braking +3% (26.3 to 27.1 m). Every metric still passes at 0.1 and 0.2 on those two; at 0.3 sports-100-rwd misses its 0-100 range. Off (0, the default) every number is bit identical to before (smoke test, engine tests). To adopt it, retune the grip up a few percent per car and re-run the metrics, laps and WASD sweeps; bars and springs then become balance knobs, so #38 no longer holds.
51. **Driving feel on the mountain pass: 50 hp more, and what did nothing (sports car).** `vehicle-feel.mjs` runs a preset down and up the pass with the keyboard driver at five paces (0.85 to 1.05 of the grip) and reports the run time, the pull (mean acceleration at full throttle between 40 and 110 km/h), the share of the run over 5° of sideslip, slides the car recovered, spins, how hard the rear tires work against the fronts, and guardrail hits; `--set` tries a car layer field without editing the presets. Means over the ten runs on sports-50-rwd: at 420 N·m the pull was 3.40 m/s², at 486 N·m (370 hp, 50 more) 3.68, and the runs are about 2 s quicker each way. That is 8% more pull from 16% more torque. Second and third gear are power limited (1.0 and 0.8 of the load), and that is where torque shows. Metrics: 0-100 4.83 to 4.18 s at 50% RWD (5.13 to 5.02 at 100%, traction limited), top speed 251 to 267 km/h, every check passing with `--perturb` and with the showcase's twin-scroll turbo. Each of these moved the pull, the times and the slides by less than the spread between runs: `shiftDownRpm` 2000 to 4600 (from 3200: it moves the time spent above 75% of the limiter, 17 to 34%, and the shifts per km, 8 to 19, but not the pull, and the gearbox only downshifts when the lower gear lands under 90% of the upshift point, so a high setting cannot over-rev), final drive 3.2 to 4.1, a taller first gear, the same power from revs (454 N·m with the limiter at 7700), `speedReference` 16, 38° of lock, rear differential 1.2 and 1.9, steering rates 6/8, front slide friction 1.1, front brakes 2900, less downforce. Engine inertia 0.12 (from 0.2) pulled 3% harder, but one perturbed arcade AWD car then swung 26° lifting mid corner (the engine braking bites sooner); 0.15 passes and measures nothing. `engine.angularDamping` 0.05 (from 0.08) pulled 7% harder: at 6900 rpm the damping takes 58 N·m, a sixth of the engine's torque there, so lowering it is more power under another name. More power costs the autopilot at its top pace on the pass: at 0.9, 26 of the 30 sports runs of the pass finish (29 at 420 N·m; the rally car, unchanged, 26), all four lost downhill at two corners (2280 m and 3070 m) where it holds full throttle into a slide. Paces 0.8 and 0.85, the keyboard driver at all three and both race tracks at all three stay clean.
52. **A looser rear at one level: the car's own `rearGrip`.** At 50% the RWD sports car was tidy to a fault: the keyboard moves peaked at 8-12° of sideslip, the WASD driver ploughed (11-15°) when it overdid a corner, and on the pass the rear worked at 0.84 of the front's slip use. Loosening the rear in the car layer livened it up but broke the arcade levels, which the realism row already loosens: rear μ 1.09 (from 1.10) put the arcade RWD and AWD cars at 27-32° lifting or flicking, 1.08 at 35-46°, a rear peak at 6° (from 5.5°) at 26-27°, rear slide friction 1.07 at 34-39°, `speedReference` 15 at 30-35°. The realism row's 50% `rearGrip` at 0.99 suited the sports car and failed the rally car's flick (25-26° in two perturbed variants; its rear is at its own edge, #46). So a car can carry its rear grip for a level: `rearGrip: { 50: 0.99 }` in the car layer is used in place of the row's value. Sports car at 50% RWD: 0.99 gives 9/10/14/11/16° in the keyboard moves (was 8/8/8/11/12°), and an overdone corner slides at 21° with two driver styles of three; 0.985 gives 9/10/18/12/19°; 0.98 gives 22° and overdone slides of 27-36°. All three pass `--perturb`. The laps chose 0.99: with 0.985 two 50% cars rolled landing the circuit's big hump at pace 0.9 and two more spun on the pass downhill. On the pass with the extra power (means of ten keyboard runs against the old car): share of the run over 5° of sideslip 5.8 to 6.8%, slides recovered 3.1 to 4.7 a run, spins 0.4 to 0.2, rear against front 0.84 to 0.87, guardrail hits 3.5 to 4.5 (none at 0.85 and 0.9; pushed harder, the faster car arrives harder).
53. **The racing line: a fast bend runs out of lock before it runs out of grip.** `buildRaceTrack` now gives every track a racing line (K1999: each point moves across the road until its curvature is the mean of its neighbours'), and `vehicle-laps.mjs --line` drives it. Three things it taught about the cars. (a) *Steering at speed.* On the pass the line turns a 20 m kink into a 70-100 m sweep taken at about 100 km/h. The sports car left the road there with 1.2 g planned and 1.43 g of grip: at 28 m/s `speedReference` 14 leaves 34° ÷ (1 + (28 ÷ 14)²) = 6.8° of lock, and the bend takes 1.7° for its radius plus the front slip angle, 7° at the peak and about 4.6° at that cornering force. So above about 100 km/h the corner speed is set by the lock, not the tires, and `racingLineSpeeds` solves for the speed where the lock left is zero. A keyboard player meets the same limit. (b) *A fixed steering gain.* The autopilot's steer input was 2.5 × the heading error, so 14° of error asked for 61% of those 6.8°. On the line the driver steers by the wheel angle the path needs, as a share of the lock at that speed. On the centreline neither showed: there was half a road to run wide on. (c) *Braking and cornering share the tires.* Planning the braking at a flat 0.8 of the grip while the corner already tightens put the car wide at the turn-in point; the plan now takes the cornering force out first (a friction circle). Minimising the summed squared second differences of the points instead of matching curvature gave a line that hugged the inside of every hairpin (4.3 m against the centreline's 6.2 m): points bunch on the inside, and closer points read as smoother. With all three in, sports-50-rwd runs the circuit in 46.4 s against 51.8 s on the centreline and the pass downhill in 237 s against 269 s; over the 45 presets the line finishes 268 of 270 laps on the circuit and stage and 259 of 270 runs on the pass. The 50% and 75% rally AWD cars run wide under power out of one left hander downhill at every pace, which no plan of speeds along a line knows.
54. **Tire compounds: what a hairpin at 50 km/h takes, and why a slip angle is slow.** The pass has 13 hairpins with racing line radii of 7.3 to 14.3 m, so 50 km/h takes 1.4 to 2.7 g; the 50% sports car on its road tire (1.43) took them at 26 to 37 km/h. Games: Assetto Corsa compounds differ by peak and peak angle (street 1.23 at 9°, semi slick 1.28 at 8°, slick 1.5 at 7°) and all keep about 90% far past the peak; GTA V runs 2.0 to 2.65 with 80 to 96% left sliding, and its drift tune keeps 99.7% over a 27° window with 2.4 times the drive force. `wheeledVehicleCompounds` holds whole tires in that spirit, and the sports car first fitted a 3.0 `touge` (9°/7°, no falloff) at 100% and 50%; see #55 for what it became. What about 250 U-turn runs on flat ground and the pass itself showed: (a) *The tire is not enough.* With 3 g of tire and nothing else the car still made 43 km/h in the tightest hairpin. It needs lock (`speedReference` 14 leaves 17° at 50 km/h and a 7.3 m radius takes 20°; 20 fits) and it must stay on its wheels (it lifts the inside pair at about 2.8 g). (b) *Lower the center of mass, do not raise `rollInfluence`.* `rollInfluence` 0.5 or 0.8 kept the car flat and made brake-and-turn from 110 km/h spin to 85°: it takes the load off the outside rear, as the realism table's comment says. A center of mass 0.25 m below the car's own with `rollInfluence` 0.15 passes every keyboard move at both levels. 0.15 m passed the flat tests and then rolled the 100% car in the pass's tightest hairpin at 44 km/h, so check roll on the pass, not only on flat ground; 0.35 m made the 50% car slide to 31° in a flick. (c) *A large slip angle is slow whatever the curve.* Curves peaking at 13 to 15°, or rising all the way to 25°, dropped the car to about 30 km/h in the hairpin: the side force is square to the wheel, so at a slip angle part of it points backwards (2.7 g at 13° is 0.6 g of drag), and traction control leaves the cornering rear tire almost no drive. A tire curve can stop punishing a slide (no falloff, a later peak); it cannot make one faster. That takes drive or a force along the path while sliding, which is engine work. (d) *The autopilot overdrives it.* The lap drivers plan from the front tire's peak friction; on `touge` the car reaches about 2.7 of 3.0 g, and at pace 0.85 the centreline driver cuts the uphill hairpins onto the inside bank. All six presets finish the pass both ways at 0.7 and downhill at 0.85 with every driver. `vehicle-metrics.mjs` scales its grip targets by the compound over the road tire and skips ordering checks across tires.
55. **Steering lock at speed must match the side grip, and a rear that grips harder sliding is what makes a slide catchable.** Driven by hand, 3.0 of side grip was too much and 1.5 "seemed good", but with the rest of the 3 g compound left in place the car was hard to catch: on the keyboard moves it reached 13/67/17/80/87° (brake and turn / trail brake / lift off / slalom / flick) against 9/10/14/11/16° on the road tire. One change at a time from there: `speedReference` 20 → 14 alone gave 13/13/8/12/8 (17 still spun the flick, 59°); rear `latSlide` 110% of its peak 13/14/11/37/40 and 120% 13/13/10/28/26; `counterSteer` 0.2 → 0.4 13/14/12/47/35; the level's own center of mass 14/13/7/45/40. A front that loses grip past the peak (96%), an earlier peak on both axles, a later rear peak and less forward grip all made it worse or changed nothing. So the cause was the steering: `speedReference` 20 was sized for 3 g, and with half the grip it turns the fronts in harder at speed than the rear can follow. `touge` is now 1.5 at 9°/7° with `speedReference` 15 and the rear at 110% sliding: 10/12/10/8/6 at 50% RWD and 13/12/8/11/7 at 100%, and no spin in ten keyboard runs of the pass at either level (the 100% car, with no counter steer assist, slides about 8 times a run, to 21°). The 0.25 m center of mass drop went back to 0.1: at 1.5 g it is not needed, and with it the 100% car rolled 1.0° in the step steer where a realistic car must roll 1.4°. *Braking* is the forward grip: with ABS the car stops at the longitudinal peak, and the compound sizes the brake torque to it. 2.35 → 3.0 took 100-0 from 16.9 to 13.4 m with the keyboard moves unchanged or better (3.6 gives 11.4 m; at the final center of mass height it is 14.8 m at 50% and 13.7 m at 100%); the sports car is power limited, so forward grip adds no wheelspin and 0-100 stays 3.82 s. Driven by hand, 3.0 was then too much braking, and `touge` went back to 2.35 (17.8 m at 50%, 17.2 m at 100%): 16.9 m had read as too little and 14 m as too much, so the window a player accepts is narrow; 2.35 was then too much as well, and forward grip settled at 1.5, the same as the side grip (26 m, the road tire's braking). With those brakes the keyboard driver brushes a guardrail about 4 times a run of the pass (none at 2.35), spinning in none. When a compound's side grip changes, retune `speedReference` with it before anything else.
56. **Landings need low rebound damping, and a wall of boxes stops a car at its joints.** (a) *Landing.* The sports car "stuck to the ground" on landing: dropped from 1 m the body sank 16 cm and came back to ride height with 0.2 cm of overshoot. The bump stop takes the fall without giving anything back (the downward speed goes from 3.5 m/s to zero in one sample), so the only rebound is the spring pushing back from full compression, and `reboundDamping` 0.75 damped that out. Rebound damping against overshoot from a 1 m drop: 0.5 → 1.9 cm, 0.35 → 4.0 cm, 0.25 → 5.9 cm, 0.15 → 8.9 cm (and from 2 m the wheels leave the ground again). Compression damping and spring rate move it much less. It changes no keyboard move by more than 3°. The one place it shows is the landing off the circuit's big hump, which is a lottery at every value: laps of the 15 sports presets at paces 0.85 and 0.9 that tilt past 37° or leave the road by 8 m were 5 of 30 at 0.75, 3 at 0.5, 1 at 0.4, 1 at 0.3 and 3 at 0.25 (where the smoke test's own circuit lap ran 13 m wide and took 56 s instead of 49). The sports car has 0.3: 4.8 cm from 1 m, 6.5 cm from 2 m. A real bounce off the bump stop needed the engine: `suspension.bumpStopRestitution` (0 to 1) makes the stop give back that share of the speed the chassis met it with, where it used to hold the chassis at rest once the fall was spent. The stop's force is capped at 6 times the static load, so it pushes back over the distance that force needs for the speed, not in one step. On the sports car, rebound above ride height from 1 m and 2 m: 0 → 4.9 and 6.6 cm, 0.3 → 6.0 and 8.8 cm, 0.5 → 8.4 and 19.9 cm (0.35 s back in the air), 0.6 → 11.5 and 32.9 cm. It costs landings on the circuit's hump: laps that go wide or tilt, of 60 (15 presets, paces 0.85 and 0.9), were 1 at 0, 3 at 0.3, 6 at 0.4 and 5 at 0.5. The sports car has 0.5. The default car hardly reaches its stops from 1 m (its spring and damper take the fall first), so bounce is tested from the height that bottoms the car out. (b) *Guardrails.* A wall made of one hull per 2 m stops a car that brushes it: the chassis corner sinks a little into one hull and meets the end face of the next, head on. On a straight wall at 80 km/h a 5° brush went to 0 km/h at 127 g; on the real downhill 22 of 28 hits at 10 and 20° stopped the car. Friction is not the cause (0 instead of 0.1 changed which angles stopped, not whether). Capsules end to end, sharing their end points, have no end faces: the same hits keep 52 to 79 km/h. Use capsules (or one hull per straight) for anything a car slides along.
57. **Opposite lock runs out at speed: `counterSteerLock`.** The speed falloff is the same both ways, so with `speedReference` 15 full opposite lock at 90 km/h is 9°, less than a 20° slide: the driver cannot point the wheels where the car is going, and lowering the falloff to gain it brings back the turn-in that spins the car (#55). The engine now adds lock only when the input steers into the rear slide: `counterSteerLock` × input × (slide angle − the rear tires' peak slip angle). A catch test (handbrake throw to 20 or 35°, then full opposite lock with keys through the limiter until the slide is under 4°): at 110 km/h from 20°, the 50% car is caught in 1.93 s after swinging to 53° without it, 0.68 s at 0.5 and 0.62 s at 1; the 100% car catches the 35° throws at 90 and 110 km/h only with it. Two traps. (a) *Counting the whole slide angle* over-rotated every RWD and AWD car in the slalom move (38° against 13°): each change of direction steers the way the rear is still sliding, and got extra lock for it. Counting only the slide past the rear peak angle fixed that on `touge` (rear peak 7°) but not on the road tire (5.5°): arcade RWD 29° in a perturbed slalom, 75% RWD 41°. So it sits on the compound. (b) *A keyboard over-corrects with it.* Ten runs of the pass with the keyboard driver: 50% car 0 spins at 0 and 0.5, 2 at 1; 100% car (no counter steer assist) 1 spin at 0, 4 at 0.5, 3 at 1. `touge` has 0.5. It is a help for a player who lifts off the lock in time, and a cost for one who holds it.
58. **Rounded tire curves: the steeper start is the whole effect, so move the peak with it.** `tire.curveRounding` 1 draws each curve as a cubic up to the peak (1.5x − 0.5x³, 1.5 times the straight line's slope at zero, level at the peak) and a smoothstep from the peak to the slide value. Peak and slide values stay put, so lateral g, braking and 0-100 do not move; what changes is how hard the car answers the steering below the limit. On `touge` with the peaks left at 9° and 7°: brake and turn 21 to 25° (10 before) and the keyboard driver failed 2 of 10 runs of the pass. Nothing else fixed that (a later front peak alone, `speedReference` 14, more rear slide grip, rounding 0.7). Moving both peaks later did: at 12° and 9° the start is 12% steeper than the straight 9° line was, the last 15% of the grip arrives over the last third of the angle, the keyboard moves read 15/13/10/11/12 and all 10 runs finish. A slide counter with a fixed sideslip threshold reads higher on a later peak (154 against 40 slides in ten runs) without the car being any looser: it corners at more slip angle by design. ABS takes the share of side grip in use from the same curve.
59. **Shifts: rev match, and what taking traction control off costs.** (a) The gearbox opened the clutch and cut the throttle for `shiftTime`, then gave the throttle back at once while the clutch closed over `clutchReleaseTime`. With 0.12 s and the sports car's light engine the revs barely fell in the shift, flared on the returning throttle (6540 → 6650 rpm) and were dragged to the next gear by the clutch; a downshift closed the clutch on an engine 700 to 1500 rpm too slow, which brakes the driven wheels. `transmission.revMatch` moves the engine to the next gear's speed while the clutch is open and brings the throttle in with the clutch: the 2 to 3 upshift now falls from 6530 straight to 4230 rpm (third needs 4360) and the downshift jerk drops to a third. The sports car has 0.3 s + 0.25 s, about 0.5 s without drive per shift, which with the grip below moves 0-100 from 4.2 to 5.3 s. (b) `touge` ended at 1.3 of grip both ways and no traction control, both asked for after driving it. Without traction control the moves made at full throttle are no longer forgiving on RWD (lift off 27°, slalom 30°, flick 88° at 50%), and the keyboard driver on the pass finishes 5 of 10 runs with 12 spins (10 of 10 and 1 with it on); FWD and AWD stay under 21°. The metrics script skips those three moves for a compound without traction control and still holds brake and turn and trail braking to 25°. (c) Bump stop restitution settled at 0.4: at 0.5 one perturbed arcade AWD preset went over on the rough ground test (upright 0.24), and at 100% the compound keeps the center of mass 0.05 m down, the least that kept the AWD car upright there.
60. **The compound layer is gone: the setup lives on the cars, and what carried over to the rally car and the supercar.** Findings #54 to #59 speak of a `touge` compound, a second tire fitted at 100% and 50% in absolute values. It is folded into the sports car's own layer (the 50% preset builds to the same values; 100% now has 1.0 of grip, not the 1.3 the compound gave it) and `wheeledVehicleCompounds`, `applyWheeledVehicleCompound` and the `compound` argument no longer exist. No realism level forces traction control any more. Carrying the treatment to the other cars: (a) *Grip does not carry.* The sports car's 9% cut, applied to the supercar, put it at 1.09 g at 100% against a class floor of 1.1, and its braking and turn-at-speed out of range too; the rally car at 50% already had 1.3. Both keep their grip. (b) *Peak angle carries as a ratio, about a third later for the rounded curve* (sports 9 → 12 was that step; rally 11 → 14.5, supercar 6.5 → 8.5).
