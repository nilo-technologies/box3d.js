# Findings

What tuning the 45 car matrix taught, with the measurement that showed it. Newest at the bottom. Add to this file when you learn something new.

## Testing

1. **The lateral grip test must turn off the speed sensitive steering.** With `speedReference` 11 m/s the steering at 70 km/h is capped at about 8°, and the front tires need about 7° of slip at the peak, so lateral g measured the steering, not the tires: the arcade sports car read 1.17 g, less than the 50% car (1.32 g). With `speedReference` 0 during the test it reads 1.61 g and the realism ordering holds.
2. **The chassis is one box with no cabin.** A car dropped on its side tips back onto its wheels without any self righting, because the box is only 0.5-0.7 m tall. Test self righting from the roof instead.
3. **A dead level roof leaves the righting direction undecided.** At exactly 180° the roll angle flips between +π and -π every step and the torques cancel, so even `selfRighting` 10 stayed on its roof. Tilt the roof test by about 6°, as a real landing would be.
4. **Handbrake tests need full steer.** At 0.4 steer and 60 km/h every level rotated at about 33°/s, which is just cornering. At full steer the realistic sports car peaks at 78°/s and the arcade one at 170°/s.
5. **Lift before braking.** Lifting the throttle at 6000 rpm sends a -1000 N·m engine braking pulse into the driven wheels. Landing it in the same frames as full brake pushed the front wheels of a FWD supercar past their peak slip, and ABS never recovered them (37.4 m instead of 34.3 m). The metrics lift for 0.25 s first, like a driver.

## Engine behavior to design around

6. **The automatic gearbox shifts on road rpm, which trails the engine.** The shift uses the lower of engine rpm and wheel speed through the gearing. At full load the clutch slips about 300 rpm, so with `shiftUpRpm` 8200 and `maxRpm` 8500 the supercar sat on the limiter in 5th at 277 km/h. At 7900 it shifts and reaches 338 km/h. Keep `shiftUpRpm` at least 5% under `maxRpm` (and see #24: the shift points are now at 90%).
7. **ABS cannot release a locked wheel.** ABS caps the brake at the tire's peak torque, but a locked wheel needs only the lower sliding torque to stay locked, so a wheel that overshoots its peak slip (a full brake step before the weight has moved forward, or an engine braking pulse) stays locked. A front brake far above the front tires' peak makes this likely: the arcade AWD supercar braked in 24.3 m with 3200 N·m fronts and 22.2 m with 2400 N·m. Size front brakes near 1.15x the front peak torque on cars with ABS. (This is an engine limitation worth fixing in box3d: ABS should release when the slip passes the peak.)
8. **Traction control is conservative on a launch.** It caps the engine at the driven tires' peak torque, but part of that torque spins up the engine's own inertia and some goes to engine friction, so in first gear the tire ran at about 60% of its peak with no slip at all. The 50% rally car with TC took 6.47 s to 100 km/h while the 75% one without it (and with less grip) took 5.27 s. Lower engine inertia helps a little (0.15 to 0.1: 6.50 to 6.25 s). Expect two wheel drive cars with TC to launch slower than the tires allow.
9. **Full throttle without TC spins the driven tires to about 50 km/h** on a 420 N·m RWD car (slip ratio 7 at launch), so the tire works at its sliding friction. That is the realistic result for a flat out launch without launch control.
10. **The yaw inertia comes from the collision box.** `mass` rescales the shapes' inertia, so a longer or wider `halfExtents` makes the car rotate more lazily. Moving the center of mass does not change the inertia.
11. **Downforce acts at the center of mass** with forward speed squared: it adds grip at both ends equally and cannot balance the car. It also adds rolling resistance (the top speed fell 2-3% at the arcade levels).

## Tuning

12. **Body roll**: 22000/18000 N/m bars on a 1400 kg car with 2.0/2.2 Hz springs gave 1.3° in a 0.9 g step steer (too stiff for a road car); 16000/13000 gave 1.7°.
13. **FWD needs weight on the front.** Moving the center of mass 0.15 m forward took the FWD sports car from 7.05 s to 6.32 s and the realistic FWD roll into range.
14. **counterSteer fights the handbrake.** Above about 0.6 it catches the slide faster than `driftGripScale` lets the rear go: arcade rotated less than 25% until counterSteer went 0.7 to 0.6 and driftGripScale 0.45 to 0.4.
15. **A taller body needs more self righting.** `selfRighting` 3 rights the sports car (box half height 0.28 m) from its roof but not the rally car (0.34 m); 4 does both.
16. **Robustness check**: after tuning, ±3% mass and ±2% grip on all 45 presets flipped no checks. Run a similar check after big changes, because ABS lockups and spins are threshold effects and a preset that only just passes can flip.
17. **Only about 58% of the engine power reaches the drag at top speed.** Sports car: 236 kW at 6301 rpm in 6th against 139 kW of drag at 253 km/h. Supercar: 476 kW against 274 kW at 323 km/h. Rolling resistance (raised by downforce), wheel and engine damping and clutch slip take the rest. The plain (P ÷ drag)^(1/3) estimate overshot by 15-20% in the blind tests.
18. **Blind test, first round** (three agents with only this skill): a 50% AWD rally car and an arcade RWD supercar passed every check on the first run. A 75% FWD hot hatch failed braking on the first run (36.5 m against 36.4 m, with 2400 N·m front brakes, about 1.55x the front peak) and then sat on the top speed floor; it took six runs. That led to the brake sizing rule against the level's grip, the 0.58 power factor, the gear and rpm in the top speed output, `--perturb`, and gear ratios for every archetype.
19. **Blind test, second round** (fresh agents, same briefs, the skill after round one): all three cars passed every check on the first measurement and every perturbed variant (95/95, 95/95, 75/75). The top speed estimate landed within 1 km/h for the supercar and the hatch. Round two found that the brake rule was too cautious as a cap (a rally car stopped 2.1 m shorter with the archetype's 2300 N·m than with 1550), so it is now a starting point that the measurement adjusts.
20. **After merging the box3d update** (hit material friction, touchdown load, reverse traction control, brake to reverse): all 45 presets still pass, but one perturbed variant does not. The 100% FWD supercar with 2% less grip brakes in 35.1 m against 35.0. Its driven front wheels lock at the brake step (findings #7), and no preset knob changed that: front brake 2100-2400 N·m, tire peak slip 0.09-0.11, engine inertia and damping, clutch strength, shift points and wheel inertia all left it within 0.3 m. It needs the ABS fix in box3d, not tuning. New `assists.brakeReverse` (default on) makes the brake select reverse once stopped with the automatic gearbox; the metrics stop measuring before that.
21. **Sharp bumps are an engine limit, not a tuning one.** The track's old speed bumps were 14 cm capsule ridges. Every car took 10-18 g spikes at 30 km/h, bottoming its suspension, and at 45-60 km/h most were launched or flipped, rally and off-roader included. The suspension pushes along the contact normal, which on the face of a sharp ridge tilts about 45°, so the hit kicks the car up and back at once. No setting fixed 45 km/h and up. A real speed hump (8 cm high, 3.7 m long, rounded) gives 0.5-1.3 g at up to 60 km/h on every preset, so the track now has those. Test bump comfort on hump shaped obstacles; a sharp ridge measures the engine's contact model.
22. **Lower compression damping than rebound** (sports 0.3/0.75, super 0.35/0.8, rally 0.3/0.7) took the stiff cars' hump peaks at 60 km/h from 0.83 to 0.65 g (sports) and 1.27 to 1.01 g (super) with every metric still passing.
23. **Keep `minLength` well above `radius - (box half height - wheel y)`.** Shortening the sports car's `minLength` from 0.17 to 0.07-0.12 m to make room for bumps made the 100% car roll off its roof: upside down, the wheel's ground probe starts close to the roof line, overlaps the ground under it, and the suspension pushes the car over. Raising `maxLength` for room also lowers the automatic center of mass relative to the body (it follows the axle), which can put it below the box.
24. **Shift at 90% of the limiter, measured on the engine.** Racing games upshift at about 90% of the rev limit. With `shiftUpRpm` set to 0.9 × `maxRpm`, the engine still reached 94-96% before each shift, because the gearbox reads road rpm and the clutch slips 270 rpm (sports, rally) to 490 rpm (supercar) at full load; the old 6900 rpm setting on the sports car shifted at 99%. Settings of 6200, 6500 and 7150 rpm put every gear's shift at 90.1-90.4% of 7200, 7500 and 8500. The metrics `shiftShare` check now measures it. A car without TC spinning its tires in first gear still hits the limiter first: the gearbox holds the upshift until the driven wheels grip.
25. **A limited slip front axle pulls under wheelspin.** Shifting earlier left the 100% FWD sports car (no TC) spinning its fronts into second gear, and with a 1.15 front LSD it veered 5.8 m over a 200 m flat out run (the smoke test allows 5). Open (1.0) it runs within 0.2 m; 1.3 gives 3.9 m. The rally FWD cars (1.3) drift 3.5 m at 100% and 1.4 m at 75%, inside the limit. The pull is always to the same side, so part of it may be solver order rather than physics.
26. **Race tracks: what made cars crash that was not their tuning** (`examples/src/vehicle-tracks.js`, checked by `examples/scripts/vehicle-laps.mjs`). A landscape averaged over 30 m around a steep, twisting road left a 45 degree bank 6 m beside a hairpin; averaging over 12 m near the road (40 m further out) keeps the ground within 15 m of the road under 35%. A crest 30 m up passing within 50 m of the start straight made a 22 m cliff between them, so keep high sections away from low ones. Bumps that stop at the road edge are walls to a car rejoining from the verge: ramp them down beyond it. A short, steep ledge (25 cm over 1.5 m) launches cars into whatever follows: give ramps 2 m or more and leave landing room. Off the end of the terrain cars fall forever: bank the edge and wall it. With those fixed, all 45 presets lap both tracks without flipping, at the autopilot's pace and 6% either side of it (`--pace 0.8` and `--pace 0.9`; the default is 0.85), taking the stage's bump section at 60 km/h.
27. **Slip ratio near rest needs relaxation.** The slip ratio divides the wheel and ground speed difference by the ground speed, so 0.05 s after full throttle the 100% RWD sports car's rears read 33x their peak slip at 0.6 km/h. `tire.longitudinalRelaxationLength` 0.3 m makes the same launch read 0.3x, 1.5x, 3.2x over the first 0.2 s. It does not stop wheelspin on a car with twice its rear grip in first gear (still spins to 78 km/h without TC), and allows 5-10% more throttle before the tires break loose. The relaxation must settle at a minimum speed (1 m/s): with the driven floor of 0.1 m/s a slip left from a spike at 1 km/h took seconds to decay, and launch control read it as wheelspin and cut the drive for 0.4 s.
28. **Launch control** (on by default). Full throttle from rest, before and after: sports-100-rwd spun to 79 km/h, 5.65 s to 100 km/h, now no wheelspin and 5.00 s; sports-100-fwd 92 km/h, 6.60 s, now 5.85 s; rally-100-rwd 5.65 s, now 5.15 s; super-100-rwd (TC on, no spin) 3.62 s, now 3.17 s; the FWD example (TC on) 9.68 s, now 7.98 s. Worst slip 1.0-1.5x the peak. Three designs failed first: an integrating cap (overshot to 7-10x the peak slip and launched slower than TC), a cap up to 2x the peak torque at the wheel (a light wheel spins up within one step on torque past the tire's peak), and ending at the first upshift (the gearbox cuts the throttle during a shift). After it, `--all --perturb` fails 7 of 4329 checks: super-0-fwd is now quicker than its target (0-100 3.82-3.93 s against a 4.00 s floor, in the preset and four perturbed variants), super-50-fwd rolled once on the rough ground test (its perturbed variants pass, and it rolls at 12.6 s, long after launch control ended at 4.4 s: the quicker start puts it on a different line through the bumps), and the known super-100-fwd grip-2% braking (#20).
29. **The 0-100 targets after launch control.** The 0.6 s two wheel drive slack is gone: it stood for the time lost without launch control, and with it the slowest two wheel drive car (sports-50-rwd grip-2%) runs 5.32 s against a 5.40 s ceiling. The FWD supercar floor at 100% is 4.7 s instead of 5.0: with the tires at their peak the 100% preset runs 6.2 s and the arcade one 3.8-3.9 s, under the old arcade floor of 4.0 s (5.0 × 0.8).
30. **Braking in a turn spun the 50% and arcade cars.** A keyboard player brakes and steers at once, so the metrics now run full brake and full lock from 110 km/h (`brakeTurn`). Before this, sports-50 reached 110-116° of sideslip, sports-25 and arcade 155-162°, even with ABS on and the steering limiter of the examples. The 100% car stayed at 9°. Two causes, both measured per wheel. ABS caps the brake at the tire's straight line peak and ignores the cornering force, so in a turn the front wheels lock within 0.3 s at every level (slip ratio -1), and the inside rear locks next. Then the outside rear is all that holds the car, and `rollInfluence` takes load off it: on sports-50 at 0, 0.1, 0.2 and 0.35 the sideslip was 7°, 11°, 47° and 116°. Moving the rear brake toward the ideal split (1500 to 900 N·m on the sports car, about 26% of the braking) and scaling it with the level's grip, with `rollInfluence` at 0.1-0.25 instead of 0.15-0.75, holds every sports preset under 20°. Braking distances moved by under 0.5 m. Lowering the rear brake alone was not enough at 25% (132° at 900 N·m with 0.55 `rollInfluence`), and 550 N·m cost 5 m of braking on the FWD and AWD cars. The real fix is ABS that leaves room for the cornering force, as traction control already does (`lateralRoom` in `vehicle.c`).
31. **counterSteer works in every corner, so it costs turning.** It adds the rear axle's slide angle to the steer target, and in a normal corner the rears run at 3-5° of slip angle, so 0.6 took about 2.5° off the 7° the fronts needed: the arcade car turned at 0.8 g at full lock and 120 km/h with 1.6 g of grip. Without it (0) the RWD 50% car spun in the step steer at 80 km/h. Stability from the tires works better than counter steer (see #33 for which kind). `counterSteer` was also what straightened a car after a handbrake slide, so with less of it the handbrake recovery test (not a metric) peaks at 25-45° instead of 12-20°, and still recovers on its own.
32. **75% without traction control is on a knife edge.** sports-75-rwd spun in the slalom (180°) with a rear grip bias of 1.05 and held 13° with 1.1; with no bias and grip 1.15 it spins in the flick (180°). Its full throttle step steer at 80 km/h spins in some settings and not others; the level may spin, so the metrics only check it at 50% and below. Check 75% RWD cars by hand with the keyboard moves.
33. **A rear only grip bias stabilises the car but kills drifts.** Rear lateral friction ×1.15-1.2 and a rear peak angle ×0.75-0.8 kept every keyboard move under 6° on the 50% sports car with 30-40% more steering at speed, but cut the handbrake rotation by 20% at 50% (148° to 117°, 134 to 108°/s) and 15% at 25%. Dividing `driftGripScale` by the bias won back only half, because the grippier rear straightens the car as soon as the handbrake is released. More grip on all four tires keeps the balance and the drifts (sports-50-rwd 147°, 133°/s; arcade 170°, 209°/s against 170°/s before). With no bias, more steering at speed is only safe from 25% down: at 50% ×1.15 the RWD car spun lock to lock (49-52°), and at arcade ×1.2 with grip 1.55 it spun in the flick (51°). Arcade grip 1.6 with ×1.15 holds every perturbed variant under 12°.
34. **Braking and steering together was set by ABS, not the presets.** With full brake and full lock the front wheels of every sports preset stayed locked for 93-96% of the stop, ABS on, so the car ran wide (1.0-1.7°/m of turn from 110 km/h). Front brakes ×0.8 changed nothing; ×0.65 and ×0.5 unlocked them and doubled the turn, but the car then spun (60-75° of sideslip), because the braked rears had no grip left for the cornering force, and weaker rear brakes as well (×0.5, ×0.35) still spun from 110 km/h. One brake torque cannot be right for a straight stop and a braking turn, so ABS in `vehicle.c` changed (#35).
35. **ABS with cornering room, release and engine braking.** Three parts, each found by measuring. (a) The ABS cap is the peak torque times the room on the friction ellipse, sqrt(1 - use²) with a 30% floor. (b) A wheel past its peak slip under braking is released to half its sliding torque, as traction control already does for wheelspin; this ends the lockups of #7 and #20. (c) Engine braking on a driven wheel shares the cap: without it the 50% RWD sports car braking mid corner spun at 124°, because the service brake was capped but the rears still carried 2300 N of engine braking. The cornering use must come from the slip angle (against `lateralPeakAngle`), not from last step's lateral force: a wheel braking hard carries little lateral force, which read as room to brake harder, and the arcade RWD supercar's rear braking force swung between 3000 and 10700 N and spun it at 168°. With all three, the fronts of every sports preset never lock in a full brake at full lock (0% of the stop), the car turns 60-80% more per metre (sports-50: 1.46 to 2.32°/m from 110 km/h) at 12-15° of sideslip, and straight line ABS braking is unchanged (the engine test: 32.2 to 32.1 m). Weaker front brakes on top of it spun the car again (×0.65: 50-70°), so the preset brakes stay. All 45 presets lap both tracks at paces 0.8 and 0.9; at 0.85 one 100% sports car rolls on the gravel stage, at that pace only (0.82-0.88 pass): it brakes for the bump section from about 98 km/h and lands on two wheels. Which one moves with every change (FWD after the ABS change, RWD after #37), so treat it as the autopilot meeting that section at a knife edge, not as a car that rolls.
36. **The steering limiter decides the balance, so test with it.** The examples cap the steer angle at the front tires' peak slip angle (now `createSteeringLimiter` in `box3d.js/vehicles`). The WASD driver (`vehicle-wasd.mjs`) on the old presets with that cap: every level from 75% FWD down failed by ploughing wide at 5-10° of sideslip, and the arcade sports car was clean to 1.67 g against 2.0-2.2 g without the cap. Without it full lock at speed takes the fronts far past their peak (19° in a braking turn), and that understeer is what kept the cars from spinning. Holding the fronts at their best grip moved the balance to oversteer: the 25% RWD car braking at full lock went from 14° to 102° of sideslip through a cap at 1.5 times the peak. So the keyboard moves in the metrics now run through the limiter.
37. **WASD tuning of the sports car.** Measured as the sum of the clean pace (mean over three driver styles) over 10 presets, starting at 14.10 with the cap at 1.5. Rear tire side grip ×1.05 (`rear.tire`) made every keyboard move pass through the cap; ×1.1 made the 50% cars plough. `speedReference` 11 to 14 and lateral slide friction 0.94 to 1.02 (on both axles, the rear ×1.05 on top) raised it most (+1.3 together); `speedReference` 17 spun the cars braking into a turn. The cap at 1.0 passed every keyboard move but ploughed (13.2); at 1.25 and up braking at full lock spun the car (30-70°): a cap of 1.5, falling to 1.0 while braking (`brakingPeakScale`), did both (15.7). A realism row multiplier on the steering falloff (×1.15 at 25% and arcade) failed the slalom and flick and was dropped. Traction control on the car (it was off) took the 100% and 75% RWD cars from 1.07 and 1.27 g to 1.23 and 1.43 g, and their failure from a spin to a slide, with every check still passing. `rearGrip` 0.975 at arcade (0.985 at 25%) made the overspeed RWD car slide (16-29°) instead of plough; 0.97 failed one perturbed flick (26°), 0.98 ploughed again. Over the 15 sports presets the clean paces are now 1.10-1.23 g at 100%, 1.33-1.43 at 75%, 1.53-1.63 at 50%, 1.77-1.97 at 25% and 2.00-2.17 at arcade.
38. **What barely moves a keyboard car's cornering.** On top of #37, each within 0.1 g of the 15.93 sum (the noise): anti-roll bars 20000/13000 and 12000/10000 (from 16000/13000), springs 2.3/2.5 Hz (from 2.0/2.2), the center of mass 4 cm lower, steering lock 30° (from 34°), steering rates 5/7 rad/s (from 4/6), 470 N·m (from 420) and a 3.9 final drive (from 3.6). Tire grip is linear in load in this engine, so moving load between the axles or across them does not shift the grip balance the way it does on real tires; tune the balance with the tires.
39. **The rally car with the keyboard recipe.** WASD sum (15 presets) 18.80 before. Rear tire side grip ×1.05 fixed six of the seven lift-off spins through the limiter but turned less (18.37, two `turnsAtSpeed` failures); `speedReference` 12 to 15 and slide friction 0.90 to 0.96 gave the turning back (20.5) but left the RWD cars spinning braking mid corner and in the flick (32-47°). Neither the rear brake (1680 to 1150) nor the rear differential alone fixed that. A rear tire that peaks earlier (9° against 11°) and keeps its grip past the peak (slide friction = peak) did (367/369). It then read 1.21 g on the 100% RWD car in the lateral test, over the 1.13 ceiling, and 1.13 against 1.07 at 75% broke the realism ordering: the rear differential at 1.6 spun the inside rear on power, which yawed the car and inflated the reading. At 1.15 it read 1.02 g and at 1.1 every perturbed variant passed. Final: 1728/1728 with `--perturb`, WASD 20.94. A smaller rear bias (×1.02-1.03) made the overdone arcade car slide (15-25°) but failed the flick in 8-13 perturbed variants, so the arcade rally car runs wide at 10-14° of sideslip when overdone; its handbrake rotates it at about 200°/s.
40. **The supercar.** WASD sum 25.73 before. Its rear brake was 48% of the braking (2200/2400); 1300 N·m stopped the spins braking into a turn but braked 0.5 m long at arcade, 1500 does both. Rear tire side grip ×1.05 fixed the six lift-off spins; slide friction 1.0 to 1.15 and `speedReference` 13 to 15 took it to 28.03. Over the sweep's top (2.4 g) the arcade supercar never fails; taken far too fast (2.6-3.3 g) it ploughs at 6-10° of sideslip. A smaller rear bias brings the slides back (×1.0: 19-51°, ×1.02: 16-26°), but the 25% and arcade RWD cars then spin when lifting mid corner (34-57°), and neither less engine braking (0.08 to 0.025), a looser rear differential (1.1) nor less downforce at the arcade levels (×6 to ×2) changed that trade: overdone and lifted, a slide is a slide. The rear bias stays ×1.05.
41. **A braking move must end when the car stops.** With S held the automatic gearbox selects reverse once stopped, and a car reversing reads as 180° of sideslip. The arcade supercar stops from 110 km/h within the 2.5 s braking-turn move and read 164-168° ("spun") though its sideslip never passed 5°. The keyboard moves now end when the car drops below 1 m/s.
42. **The stage lap at pace 0.85.** Now two presets roll in the stage's bump section at that pace only (sports-100-rwd and super-50-fwd, both at 347-362 m), each passing from 0.82 to 0.88 and at 0.8 and 0.9; super-50-fwd lands back on its wheels. Which presets trip moves with every tuning change, so it is the autopilot reaching that section at one particular speed, not a property of the cars.
43. **The 0-100 floor follows the grip.** It was the archetype floor × (1 - 0.2 × arcade), set when the arcade grip was 1.45. With the arcade grip at 1.6 the FWD supercar, whose launch is traction limited, ran 3.60-3.68 s against 3.76. The floor is now the archetype floor × grip^-0.6: at the old grips (1.1, 1.2, 1.32, 1.45) that gives 0.94, 0.90, 0.85 and 0.80, the old floors within 0.01; at the new ones 0.92, 0.85, 0.80 and 0.75. All 45 presets then pass every check with `--perturb`.
