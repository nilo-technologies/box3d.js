# Changelog

## Unreleased

- The V10 voice is rebuilt as a race-bred V10 with ten build options. The other voices sound exactly as before.
  - The flat-plane V8's four that make it less of a machine: `combustion` (0.04), `heat` (0.25), `turbulence` (0.12) and `tolerance` (0.04). Its harmonic-to-noise ratio at speed drops from 19-24 dB to 10-12 dB.
  - `itb` (0.5): a trumpet per cylinder, as on the Lexus LFA. `flap` (5000 rpm): a bypass flap past the muffler. `blowdown`: rounded pulses with a scavenging dip.
  - `steepen` (0.5) and `spacing` (1.1 m), now in use: high pressure runs faster down the pipes, so pulse fronts sharpen toward a shock on a firm throttle, and each bank's tailpipe reaches the far ear later than the near one.
  - `megaphone` (0.4 m), new: each bank's collector widens like a cone in four sections, from 1.2 to 4 times a runner's width, rather than meeting the mid pipe at a sharp junction. Each step reflects a little, so the suction wave back up the runners is soft and spread out. It strengthens the mids from about 4500 rpm.
  - The level is 7 (was 8.7), so a drive through the gears is as loud as before, and the saturator doesn't clip.

- The flat-plane V8 voice sounds less like a machine. Every firing used to be nearly the same and the pipes were fixed filters, so at speed its harmonic-to-noise ratio was 21-25 dB. With four new build options it is 12-16 dB, the range of the rotary and the boxer. The other voices sound exactly as before.
  - `combustion` (0.04): combustion varies from cycle to cycle at any revs and load, about 4% in strength and about 1.2° in timing. The voice's jitter still fades out with the revs on top of it.
  - `heat` (0.25): each pulse is a slug of hot gas, and sound runs faster in it. It warms its runner, then its bank's mid pipe, and cools within milliseconds, so the pipes' resonances move with every pulse.
  - `turbulence` (0.12): noise where the runners' flows mix at the collector, as strong as the flow arriving, so it rides on the pulses and rings the exhaust's own resonances. It stays out of the top octaves.
  - `tolerance` (0.04): each runner is up to 4 cm longer or shorter and each cylinder up to 6% stronger or weaker, fixed per index, so the same every run. It brings up the odd orders a real engine has (orders 1 and 3 go from about -47 dB to -28 dB under the firing order).
  - The worklet also has `steepen` (shock steepening: high pressure runs faster, so pulse fronts sharpen) and `spacing` (each bank's tailpipe reaches the far ear later) as build options.
  - The level is 8.5 (was 9.4), so a drive through the gears is as loud as before.

- The inline six voice is rebuilt with six build options: `blowdown`, `drift` (0.15), `chamber` (0.35 m), `secondary` (0.5 m) and two new ones. The other voices sound exactly as before.
  - `secondary` on a six is a split manifold: firing order 1-5-3-6-2-4 alternates the front three and the rear three, so the pairing of alternate firings puts each half into a pipe of its own, and the two meet at the collector, as on a BMW six. It fills out the growl from 3000 to 4500 rpm.
  - `itb` (0.5): individual throttle bodies. Each cylinder breathes through a short trumpet of its own (0.3 m), open to the air, and its suction pulse rings it: an intake howl that grows with the revs.
  - `flap` (4000 rpm): an exhaust flap opens past the muffler on a firm throttle (from 30% to 70%) across 4000 ± 500 rpm, swinging over a quarter of a second. Through it the exhaust runs straight, with only a gentle low pass and less of the muffler's reflection. Idle and cruising are unchanged.
  - The level is 14.9 (was 11.2), so a drive through the gears is as loud as before overall, and the saturator doesn't clip.

- The inline four voice is rebuilt with six build options: the V8's `blowdown`, `drift` (0.15) and `chamber` (0.3 m), and three new ones. The other voices sound exactly as before.
  - `secondary` (0.4 m): a 4-2-1 header. Each bank's runners pair up, alternate firings together (cylinders 1 and 4, 2 and 3), into secondary pipes that meet the mid pipe at the collector: two junctions on the way out instead of one. At high revs the paired pipes ring the firing note, a fuller howl.
  - `honk` (1): the intake tract is a pipe, the runner and the airbox snorkel, closed at the valve and open at the mouth, and each suction pulse rings it. The intake honks, a tone, on top of the rushing air.
  - `cam` (5600 rpm): a second, high lift cam takes over across 300 rpm, as VTEC does. Above it the pulses are 30% shorter and 25% stronger and the intake 80% louder, so the top of each gear changes character.
  - The top end is louder against the bottom than before. The level is 12.5 (was 15.5), so a drive through the gears is as loud as before overall, and the saturator doesn't clip.

- The cross-plane V8 voice is rebuilt: it idles with a big cam's lope, and five new ways of making the sound shape its burble. The other voices sound exactly as before (they render sample for sample the same).
  - The idle lopes: the long valve overlap leaves some cylinders a diluted charge, so the idle swells and sags once every two turns, with the shaft speeding up and slowing down with it. It is the rotary's regular lope, at a gentler depth, and it fades out by 3200 rpm and on throttle. A voice's `lope` without `misfire` now means a cam lope at idle only.
  - A voice can have a `build` (`EngineBuild`), each option off unless set. The V8 uses all five:
    - `blowdown`: each exhaust pulse swells in as the valve opens and the cylinder blows down, then leaves a short suction dip as the gas rushing out scavenges the port, instead of a sharp step with an exponential decay.
    - `interference` (0.25): in a bank's shared manifold, a pulse that closely follows the last one meets its back pressure and comes out weaker, and one after a long gap comes out stronger. On a cross-plane bank (90°, 180°, 270°, 180° gaps) this is the burble, worked out from the actual gaps as the engine runs.
    - `drift` (0.15): each cylinder's strength wanders slowly from cycle to cycle, as its mixture and breathing do, on top of the per-firing randomness.
    - `crossover` (0.3): part of each bank's exhaust crosses into the other bank's pipe, as an H or X pipe joins them.
    - `chamber` (0.35 m): a chambered muffler. The wave rings between the ends of an expansion chamber on its way to the tailpipe, a bark in the mids, instead of only the muffler's low pass.
  - The V8's level is 15.8 (was 11.6), so it is about as loud as before (within about 2 dB from idle to full throttle). Its saturator no longer clips at full throttle.

- The rotary engine voice sounds like a rotary. Before, it was a soft, noisy piston engine: long blurry pulses, an even idle and a lot of hiss.
  - Its firing follows the rotors: two rotors with three faces each, every face firing once per turn of its rotor, which is three turns of the shaft. The worklet takes a cycle length per voice (`cycle`, 720° by default, 1080° for the rotary). The faces differ a little in strength, which repeats every three turns as a growl under the note.
  - Each pulse starts firmly, as the apex seal uncovers the peripheral exhaust port quickly (0.35 ms rise, was 0.8 ms), and is fat, as the port stays open long. There is less combustion noise (0.6, was 0.9) and less saturation, so the rasp is in the note rather than in hiss.
  - The idle has a brap, in a style you pick (`engine.brap`, "rotary idle (brap)" in the sound panel; `brapStyles` lists them). Each fades out above 3200 rpm and toward full throttle, so high revs stay a smooth buzz. A new `misfire` field on a voice turns this on and sets how often a firing is affected, and roughness scales it.
    - Bursts: the port overlap leaves exhaust in the chambers, a little more with each firing, so the burns weaken until a charge fails to light. Its fuel lights in the exhaust as a round whump (a slow push of pressure with a low rumble on it, which rings the pipes), and the chamber clears for a strong burn again. About four braps a second, irregularly.
    - Regular lope (the default): each face burns at a strength of its own (`lope` on the voice), strong at the start of the 1080° cycle and weaker over it, so the idle goes brap once every three turns of the shaft, about five times a second at idle, and faster with the revs. A strong burn kicks the shaft faster and the weak ones let it slow, so the pitch swings with the lope (about ±2.5% of the speed at idle). The weak faces fire up to 15° late, as diluted charges burn slowly, each face's strength varies by up to 25% from cycle to cycle so no two braps match, and the strong burns are the roughest.
    - Skipped firings: now and then a firing drops out, leaving a gap.
    - Lope with the odd skip, and Even (no brap).
  - Off idle it keeps its character. Coasting on a closed throttle above idle, the lope comes back at 80% of its idle depth (a burble) and unburnt fuel pops in the exhaust on about one firing in 25, mostly round pops with the odd crackle. Cruising on a light throttle, the lope comes through at 45%, a growl under the buzz. Flat out it keeps more of its scream: it takes half the high-rev dip around 3.2 kHz (a new `presence` field on a voice). Both fade back to a smooth pull on a full throttle.
  - The exhaust is shorter and freer: 0.3 m ports that no valve closes. The rotary has less saturation (drive 1.3, was 3), so at 6000 rpm and full throttle its saturator clips about a fifth of the time instead of three quarters. The voice is level matched to before.
  - The 3D engine fires each rotor's faces in turn over the 1080° cycle.

- The engine voices play through a model of their exhaust pipes, with intake roar, and are heard from a place: outside behind the car or in the cabin. This applies to the showcase and the sound lab.
  - The exhaust is a waveguide model in the engine worklet. Each cylinder's pulse runs down its own header runner to its bank's collector, then along a mid pipe to the muffler and out of a tailpipe. Every pipe carries a wave each way, and every end reflects part of it: the closed valves, the collector, the muffler and the open tailpipe end. The resonances come from the pipe lengths, so the boxer's unequal headers rumble, and a V engine has an exhaust per bank, with each bank's tailpipe on its own side in stereo. The gas heats under load, which raises the speed of sound and the resonances with it. Each voice has its own pipes (`EnginePipes` in `engineVoices`). Pipe size and rumble now scale the pipe lengths and how much the pipes ring. The two-resonance model is still there as `engine.exhaust: 'resonances'`.
  - Intake roar: each cylinder draws a suction pulse on its intake stroke, with rushing air on it, through the plenum and the airbox. It rises with the throttle and the airflow. `engine.intake` sets its level; the sound panel has it as "intake roar".
  - `listener.position` (new `listener` group in `VehicleSoundParams`, default `'exterior'`) picks the listener: outside behind the car (ground and wall reflections, the tailpipes apart), in the cabin (the body dulls the highs and booms at 55 Hz, more intake, a small dense room) or dry. `listener.room` sets the reflections. The room is a generated impulse response in a ConvolverNode, and `createListener(ctx)` builds the chain, so it also works in an OfflineAudioContext. `audio.output` is now after the listener. `listenerPositions` holds each position's settings.
  - Each voice's pipes and each listener position are level matched to the old sound over a drive (idle, a blip, a pull through three gears and a coast down), within about 1.5 dB. That way a before/after comparison is fair.
  - The sound lab has a before/after switch in the Play folder, also on O. It flips between the new sound and the old one (two resonances, no intake, dry) and restores your settings when you switch back. It saves nothing. The panel has the exhaust model, the intake roar and a Listener folder.
  - `engineVoiceConfig(voice, engineParams, listener)` is exported: what the worklet plays for a voice.

- The engine voices sound smoother at high revs, where they used to turn raspy and harsh. The voices are shared, so the showcase gets this too.
  - Fixed a firing bug in the engine worklet: after a cylinder fired, its random timing could put its next firing just ahead of the crank, so it fired again in the same cycle. About half the firings were doubled at random, which sounded like rasp between the harmonics and got worse with the revs (the cross-plane V8 was almost all rasp above 5000 rpm). Each firing is now scheduled a full cycle ahead. The harmonic-to-noise ratio at 7000-9000 rpm is up from 0-10 dB to 14-22 dB. Every voice's `level` is raised by 1.6 to 4.9 dB to make up for the doubled pulses, so the voices are as loud as before.
  - Each pulse now starts between samples, at the exact moment of its firing, instead of snapping to the nearest sample. The random firing timing fades as the revs and load rise, as a real engine's combustion steadies. A wide dip around 3.2 kHz deepens from 3000 to 8000 rpm, where the firing harmonics reach the range hearing is most sensitive to. The muffler rolls off at 24 dB per octave instead of 12. Together these cut the 2-6 kHz share at 8000-9000 rpm by about a factor of 2-3.
  - The turbo whistle is softer: `whineVolume` 0.012 (was 0.018), `whineTone` 0.05 (was 0.08), `whineHarmonic` 0.12 (was 0.25). The intake whoosh tops out at 2.8 kHz instead of 4 kHz. At full boost and high revs the turbo now adds 1.7 dB to the 2-6 kHz band, down from 4.4 dB.

- Vehicle wheels can be read in chassis space, every wheel in one call. `b3Vehicle_GetWheelLocalData(out, vehicleId)` fills a `Float32Array` (or a plain array) with `B3_VEHICLE_WHEEL_LOCAL_STRIDE` (11) floats per wheel, in definition order, and returns the wheel count (0 for an invalid vehicle; a RangeError when `out` is too short, and `B3_MAX_VEHICLE_WHEELS * B3_VEHICLE_WHEEL_LOCAL_STRIDE` fits any vehicle): the position (3), the rotation as x, y, z, w (4), then the raw `rotationAngle`, `angularVelocity`, `steerAngle` and `suspensionLength`. The raw spin is there so a renderer can interpolate wheels between physics steps: a fast wheel turns more than pi per step, which only `angularVelocity` can unwrap. `b3Vehicle_GetWheelLocalTransform(outPosition, outRotation, vehicleId, wheelIndex)` reads one wheel, the local counterpart of `b3Vehicle_GetWheelTransform`. The pose is the one box3d composes with the chassis transform, for any `chassis.up` and `chassis.forward`: the attachment point lowered along up by the suspension length, then steer about up times spin about the axle (up x forward). It is built in the bindings from the public chassis, wheel and wheel state reads, so `vendor/box3d` is unchanged, and it copies out of a static wasm scratch, so a read allocates nothing. The smoke test checks that the chassis transform times the local pose is the world wheel transform on a tilted, steering, moving car, with the default axes and with up +z, forward +x. The README's vehicle section shows it, interpolating the wheels between physics steps with the spin unwrapped.

- The vehicle showcase's lift sounds play only when a real car would make them, not on every lift. Before, the blow-off (or flutter) played whenever the throttle went from over half to under 10% with boost over 0.3. A keyboard throttle is all or nothing, and superchargers and the electric turbo make boost the moment the pedal is down, so nearly every lift blew off. The exhaust also crackled on every lift above 2500 rpm, for as long as you coasted. Now:
  - A blow-off or flutter needs the throttle to snap shut (from over half to under 10% within 0.3 s), boost that stayed above `blowOffMinBoost` (now 0.4) for `blowOffHold` (0.4 s), and the boost to actually fall after the lift. So taps, part throttle and slow roll-offs are quiet. A supercharger never blows off: its bypass valve recirculates quietly. Anti-lag never does either, because it holds the pressure up. A cooldown (`blowOffCooldown`, 1 s) stops quick lifts stacking. The boost at the lift sets the hiss's volume, pitch and length, and the flutter's rate and length. Upshifts on boost still blow off, as a turbo car with a manual gearbox does.
  - Overrun crackle comes after a hard pull: the exhaust heats over about 1.5 s of near full throttle above `crackleMinRpm` (3500) and cools when you ease off. A sharp lift with a hot exhaust pops with `crackleChance` (60%), in a burst that fades over `crackleLength` (1.2 s, shorter after a shorter pull) and stops if you open the throttle or the revs fall below the threshold. A burst uses up the heat, so blips after it stay quiet. Nothing pops at idle, after cruising or on taps.
  - The new thresholds are fields of `VehicleSoundParams` (`defaultSoundParams` in `examples/src/vehicle-audio.ts`). `VehicleSound` has an optional `supercharged` flag, which the showcase sets. The engine worklet takes the crackle level as an `overrun` AudioParam instead of deciding it from the throttle and rpm.

- Vehicles can have a turbo or supercharger. `b3VehicleTurbo` (in `b3VehicleDef` as `turbo`, live through `b3Vehicle_SetTurbo` / `b3Vehicle_GetTurbo`) has up to two compressor stages, each with a gain, an rpm range where its boost builds, a lag (`spoolTime`) and a shaft spin-down time (`bleedTime`), plus `instantFraction` for lag-free boost, `parasiticTorque` for a supercharger's belt drive and `antiLag`. The engine makes 1 + boost times its torque curve, and traction control and the launch clutch see the boosted torque. Exhaust energy drives the turbo, so the lag is long near the boost threshold or at part throttle, short high in the revs, and the spool up gathers pace as boost builds. Boost dumps through the blow-off valve when you lift or shift, but the shaft keeps spinning, so it comes back as soon as the throttle opens. The pedal asks for a share of the fully boosted torque: the engine makes the first part without boost, and boost builds only near full pedal. `b3VehicleState.boost` and the packed vehicle readout (`getVehicleStateAt`) report it. The default is naturally aspirated, so existing cars drive exactly as before. A `b3VehicleDef` built by hand rather than from `b3DefaultVehicleDef()` now needs a `turbo` field.
  - `box3d.js/vehicles` has nine turbo types in `turboTypes`, plus `none` for a naturally aspirated engine (small and big single, twin-scroll, parallel and sequential twin, variable geometry, electric-assisted, supercharger, rally anti-lag) as an optional fourth preset layer: `applyTurbo(def, type)`, or a fifth argument to `buildVehiclePreset`. A type keeps the car's rated peak torque, so it changes how the torque arrives, not how much there is. `vehicle-metrics.mjs --turbo <type>` measures a preset with one.

- The vehicle examples, the showcase included, can be driven on a phone or tablet. On touch screens (a coarse pointer, or the first touch) an overlay shows a steering pad on the left and gas, brake/reverse, handbrake and flip upright buttons on the right. The steering is analog, from how far out the finger is on the pad. Steering and pedals take separate fingers, and a finger can slide from gas to brake. The overlay feeds the same input as the keyboard: held buttons add their codes to the key set, the flip button sends R, and the pad steers the way the gamepad stick does. The buttons are native buttons and the pad is a slider, so they also work focused with the keyboard (Enter or Space holds a button, the arrow keys steer). While it shows, the page doesn't scroll, zoom or select text, the settings panel ends above the buttons (and starts closed on phones), the help text lists the touch controls, and the HUD and wheel load panel move clear of the buttons in portrait and landscape. Desktop is unchanged. `?touch=1` forces the overlay on to try it with a mouse, `?touch=0` keeps it off. The sound also starts when a touch lifts, which is when mobile browsers allow it. `examples/src/vehicle-touch.ts` holds the controls; the layout is in `example.css`.

New:

- Cylindrical joint: `b3_cylindricalJoint`, `b3DefaultCylindricalJointDef()`, `b3CreateCylindricalJoint(worldId, def)`. Body B rotates about frame A's x-axis and slides along `def.localSlideAxisA` (frame A x by default, which makes the joint coaxial; any other direction in frame A is allowed). The translation has a spring (`enableSpring`, `hertz`, `dampingRatio`, `targetTranslation`), limit (`enableLimit`, `lowerTranslation`, `upperTranslation`), and motor (`enableMotor`, `maxMotorForce`, `motorSpeed`); the rotation has its own (`enableAngularSpring`, `angularHertz`, `angularDampingRatio`, `targetAngle`, `enableAngularLimit`, `lowerAngle`, `upperAngle`, `enableAngularMotor`, `maxMotorTorque`, `angularMotorSpeed`). `b3CylindricalJoint_*` setters and getters, plus `GetTranslation`, `GetSpeed`, `GetAngle`, `GetAngularSpeed`, `GetMotorForce`, and `GetMotorTorque`. Debug draw shows it like the other joints.
- `b3World_SetMixingRules(worldId, frictionRule, restitutionRule)` installs native friction and restitution mixing callbacks (no JS call per contact; safe in the multithreaded builds). Rules are the plain numbers in `b3MixingRule`: `geometric` (box3d's default for friction), `average`, `min`, `max` (box3d's default for restitution), `multiply`, and `weighted`. `weighted` reads per-shape weights from each shape's `userMaterialId` (bits 0-31: friction weight × 1000; bits 32-62: restitution weight × 1000; bit 63 set: weights present) and mixes `(a·wA + b·wB) / (wA + wB)`, a plain average when both weights are 0. While `weighted` is active, `userMaterialId` holds the weights and can't double as a material tag. A shape without bit 63 falls back to box3d's default rule for that pair. An unknown rule throws a `RangeError`.
- `getBodyMoveEventsPacked(eventsBuffer)` returns every body move event of the last `getEvents` as one copied `Float64Array`, 11 numbers per event: `index1, world0, generation, px, py, pz, qx, qy, qz, qw, fellAsleep` (0/1). `b3Body_GetTransformsPacked(ids)` takes an `Int32Array` of `index1, world0, generation` triples and returns a copied `Float64Array`, 7 per body (`px, py, pz, qx, qy, qz, qw`), with `NaN` for an invalid id. Both cross the boundary once, for callers that can't read the wasm heap.
- Persistent forces: `b3Body_SetConstantForce(bodyId, force, point | null)`, `b3Body_SetConstantTorque(bodyId, torque)`, `b3Body_ClearConstantForces(bodyId)`. The bindings keep them per world and `b3World_Step` re-applies them (with `wake = false`) before every step. The force and torque are world-space; `point` is body-local, `null` means the center of mass. A new force replaces the previous one. Setting a non-zero value wakes the body once. Entries are dropped when the body is destroyed (or found invalid at step time) and when the world is destroyed.
- Distance-joint springs in stiffness units: `b3DistanceJointDef.springStiffness` and `springDamping` (default 0) and `b3DistanceJoint_SetSpringStiffness(jointId, stiffness, damping)` (`GetSpringStiffness` / `GetSpringDamping`). When either is positive they replace `hertz` / `dampingRatio`, and the spring force is exactly `-stiffness · Δlength - damping · lengthSpeed`, whatever the attached masses.
- `b3Shape_SetHull(shapeId, hull)` (1:1 with C: swaps in a copy of `hull`, mass unchanged) and `b3Shape_SetBakedHull(shapeId, hull, transform, scale, updateBodyMass)`, which swaps a shape's geometry for `hull` baked with `transform` and `scale` without a temporary hull on the JS side (the hull is copied; destroy yours when done). It returns `false`, leaving the shape unchanged, if the transformed hull is degenerate. `updateBodyMass` recomputes the body's mass from its shapes.
- `b3Shape_Disable(shapeId)` / `b3Shape_Enable(shapeId)` / `b3Shape_IsEnabled(shapeId)`. A disabled shape leaves the broad-phase: it has no contacts, sensor overlaps, or query hits until it is enabled again. Its mass still counts.
- `b3ShapeDef.meshRestOffset` (default `B3_MESH_REST_OFFSET`, 5 mm at 1 unit per meter) and `b3Shape_SetMeshRestOffset(shapeId, restOffset)` / `b3Shape_GetMeshRestOffset(shapeId)`. Mesh contacts hold other shapes this far off a mesh shape's surface; 0 lets them rest flush, so parts touching a mesh floor are not pushed up against their joints. Valid range `[0, B3_SPECULATIVE_DISTANCE)`; only mesh shapes use it.
- Natively filtered queries, for callers where a JS callback per candidate is expensive (e.g. across a sandbox): `b3QueryOptions = { ignoreBodies?: Int32Array, ignoreShapes?: Int32Array, excludeSensors?: boolean }` (id triples `index1, world0, generation`). A candidate is skipped inside the query when it or its body is listed, or it is a sensor and `excludeSensors` is set.
  - `b3World_CollideMoverPacked(worldId, origin, mover, filter, options)`: every plane of every non-skipped shape as one `Float64Array`, 14 per plane: shape id (3), body id (3), body type value, plane normal (3), plane offset, point (3, relative to `origin`).
  - `b3World_CastMoverFiltered(worldId, origin, mover, translation, filter, options)`: the `b3World_CastMover` fraction with the skipped shapes filtered out.
  - `b3World_CastRayClosestFiltered(worldId, origin, translation, filter, options)` and `b3World_CastShapeClosestFiltered(worldId, origin, points, radius, translation, filter, options)`: the closest non-skipped hit, an empty `Float64Array` on a miss, else 14 numbers: shape id (3), body id (3), point (3), normal (3), fraction, 0.

## v0.2.0

Convex decomposition: [V-HACD](https://github.com/kmammou/v-hacd) v4.1.0 is compiled into every build, so concave meshes can become collision shapes on dynamic bodies.

- `b3CreateConvexDecompositionShapes(bodyId, shapeDef, positions, indices, options?)` decomposes a mesh and attaches one hull shape per piece to the body in a single wasm call. The shape ids come back packed (`{ count, data, skipped }`, read with `getShapeIdAt`).
- `b3CreateConvexDecompositionShapesAsync(...)` is the Promise variant, and the recommended way to create these shapes. In the multithreaded builds V-HACD runs on a background thread. In browsers, the single-threaded builds run it in a Web Worker started from the module's own source, so no extra file is served; it needs `blob:` workers allowed by the page's CSP, and falls back to the main thread if they're blocked. Node runs it synchronously. The Promise rejects if the body is destroyed before the decomposition finishes.
- The async variants cache their results. An LRU of 512 entries is keyed by a 64-bit hash of the mesh, memoized per array pair, or by `options.cacheKey` (for example an asset id), plus every option that changes the output. Further bodies of the same mesh skip V-HACD: about 0.2 ms per body instead of 1.5 s for a 12-piece torus. Concurrent requests share one decomposition, and failures are not kept. Opt out with `cache: false`; inspect or reset with `b3GetConvexDecompositionCacheStats` / `b3ClearConvexDecompositionCache`; get the key itself with `b3ConvexDecompositionCacheKey`.
- `b3ComputeConvexDecomposition` / `b3ComputeConvexDecompositionAsync` return a packed decomposition (`{ points: Float32Array, offsets: Uint32Array }`) for precomputing assets offline. Rebuild shapes from it with `b3CreateConvexDecompositionShapesFromPacked`, and convert to and from one array per piece with `b3PackConvexDecomposition` / `b3UnpackConvexDecomposition`.
- `b3CreateDecompositionCompound(packed, material?)` builds a compound for static bodies.
- Pieces that box3d can't turn into a hull (hulls are capped at 255 vertices, faces and half-edges) are retried with smaller vertex budgets, and reported in `skipped` if they still fail.
- Options: `maxConvexHulls`, `resolution`, `maxVerticesPerHull`, `minVolumePercentError`, `maxRecursionDepth`, `shrinkWrap`, and `useThreads` (multithreaded builds: V-HACD's 8-thread worker pool). The integer options are clamped to the uint32 range, so `Infinity` or `NaN` can't produce an out-of-range conversion.
- `b3HasThreads()` reports whether the loaded build is multithreaded.
- The wasm grows by about 100 KiB (single-threaded) and 122 KiB (multithreaded).
- New example: Convex Decomposition. V-HACD's license is included as `LICENSE-vhacd`.

## v0.1.0

Rework of embind API surface so that box3d.js interops with gl-matrix-style libraries and reads values without allocating JavaScript objects.

- The emitted modules are now validated and post-processed into clean ESM: Emscripten's `require()` calls are rewritten to `await import`, Node builtins are given the `node:` scheme, and the single-file (`*.inline.mjs`) builds have their dead Node file-reader code stripped.
